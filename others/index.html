Adjacent experiences

Other experiences

A quiet note on adjacent fields of everyday experience.

Current version: 14 May 2026. This version is preliminary and may be refined.

Purpose

This page outlines selected adjacent fields of everyday experience beyond the primary contexts of tourism, hospitality, mobility, media, public policy and government relations.

It is intended as a low-noise orientation note. It does not represent a client list, partnership list, endorsement list or claim of formal engagement with any specific brand, company or public body.

Underlying principle

Everyday experiences often reveal how systems work in practice: operational clarity, service quality, user perception, spatial logic, communication, trust, cleanliness, packaging, access, handover moments, wayfinding and everyday usability.

The relevant question is not whether a field belongs to a specific industry category, but whether a situation contains conditions that can be observed, interpreted and translated into practical strategic value.

Adjacent experiences

  • Retail and local service experiences.
  • Food, quick service and everyday hospitality moments.
  • Consumer technology and app-based service experiences.
  • Energy, home systems and installation experiences.
  • Building, repair and maintenance situations.
  • Product interfaces, packaging and handover moments.
  • Infrastructure, signage and wayfinding outside formal mobility projects.
  • Service environments where cleanliness, trust and perceived reliability shape experience.

In short

Other experiences are noted only where structured observation may help clarify how systems, interfaces, services or everyday environments create or reduce value.
